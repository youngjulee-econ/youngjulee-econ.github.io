About

I am an applied microeconomist working on climate and energy, public, and labor economics. My research asks how people and firms actually respond when policies and institutions change, and whether those responses match what the policies intended. I study these questions with administrative data and quasi-experimental methods, and my recent work focuses on energy transition and climate policy in Korea.

Unintended Consequences of Installing Platform Screen Doors: Displacement and Substitution for Suicide Attempts

Minsung Park, Youngju Lee, and Nobuhiko NakazawaPaper
Health Economics, 35(10), 1532–1547SSCI

Physical barriers such as platform screen doors are considered effective at preventing suicides at the stations where they are installed, but whether they reduce suicide attempts overall has been unexplored. Exploiting the timing of installation and novel platform-level data on suicide attempts, we find that installing barriers causes displacement to lines without barriers in the same station and to other single-line stations, as well as substitution to other methods.

Political Alignment, Tax Evasion, and the Role of Government Credibility: Evidence from South Korea

Youngju Lee and Nobuhiko NakazawaPaper
International Tax and Public Finance, 33(4), 1449–1467SSCI

Does political alignment affect tax evasion? Using individual-level income data and county-level political data from South Korea, we show that people politically aligned with the ruling party are significantly less likely to evade taxes, with the largest effects among professionals. Political alignment is also positively related to attitudes toward government, consistent with the reciprocity view of tax morale.

The Effect of Cigarette Prices on Insurance Premiums: Evidence from Cigarette Tax Increase in South Korea

Youngju Lee and Minsung ParkPaper
Economic Analysis and Policy, 89, 1064–1076SSCI

How did South Korea’s 80% cigarette price increase in 2015 affect private health insurance premiums? Using the Korea Health Panel Survey (2010–2018) and a control function approach to address the endogeneity of smoking, we find that the policy lowered smokers’ monthly premiums by 4.7%, while smoking participation fell by 3.9 percentage points and daily consumption by 1.5 cigarettes. Tobacco taxation thus reaches private risk markets through behavioral channels.

Income Inequality Aversion and Private Charitable Contributions in Korea

Youngju Lee and Dooseok JangPaper
Global Economic Review, 53(4), 345–360SSCI

We study how income inequality aversion, a concern over income disparities, shapes private charitable donations. A dictator game with several dictators predicts that inequality aversion increases donations, that wealthy individuals can donate weakly more than the middle class, and that donations decrease as the number of people increases. We test these predictions with ten years of data on voluntary donations in Korea.

Does Single-Sex Schooling Help or Hurt Labor Market Outcomes? Evidence from a Natural Experiment in South Korea

Youngju Lee and Nobuhiko NakazawaPaper
Journal of Public Economics, 214, 104729SSCI

South Korean students are randomly assigned by lottery to single-sex or coeducational high schools within their school districts. Using this natural experiment and large individual-level panel data, we find that graduating from a single-sex school significantly lowers women’s later earnings, while the effect for men is positive but imprecisely estimated. We explore career choice and post-graduation networks as possible mechanisms and find no significant effects on other outcomes.

Spatial Effects of Air Pollution on the Housing Market: Evidence from South Korea

Jiajun Lu and Youngju LeePaper
Journal of Real Estate Research, 44(1), 131–150SSCI

We examine how ambient air pollution affects apartment values in South Korea, using detailed transaction data for 2015–2018 and a two-stage spatial Durbin error model that captures direct and spillover effects. A 1% increase in air pollution lowers a property’s value by 0.32% (about $879) on average, and the direct effect is larger in urban than in rural markets. Rising pollution in urban centers also raises housing prices in suburban and rural areas, pointing to strong spillovers and possible migration toward cleaner air.

Determinants of Interest Rate Thresholds for Mortgage Borrowers’ Transition to Digital Financial Channels

Youngju Lee and Minsung ParkPaper
Journal of Housing and Urban Finance, 11(1), 27–44KCIin Korean

How large an interest-rate discount do mortgage borrowers require to switch from face-to-face to digital channels? Using the Korea Housing Finance Corporation’s 2022–2023 survey, we find that the required differential varies systematically with region, housing tenure, income, and household structure. Non-metropolitan residents accept smaller differentials than Seoul residents, while jeonse tenants, lower-income households, and single-person households demand larger ones.

An Empirical Analysis of Housing Discrimination Experiences and Determinants among Immigrants in Korea

Youngju LeePaper
Asia-Pacific Journal of Convergent Research Interchange, 12(2), 161–174KCIin Korean

This paper studies both the incidence and the intensity of housing discrimination that immigrants in Korea experience from landlords, using the Immigrants’ Living Conditions and Labor Force Survey (2018–2024) and an ordered logit model. Employment status, health, Korean proficiency, length of stay, household type, and income all shape the risk of severe discrimination, and employed immigrants are more likely to report it. The risk peaks around three to five years of residence and then declines as social networks and trust accumulate.

Factors Associated with Career Attrition Intention and Wage Expectations among Cybersecurity Professionals-in-Training: Evidence from Cybersecurity Major Students

Youngju Lee and Jeyong JungPaper
The Journal of Police Policies, 40(2), 271–302KCIin Korean

Using student data from the 2024 Cybersecurity Workforce Supply and Demand Survey (N = 976), we examine factors associated with cybersecurity majors’ intention to leave for non-security IT careers and with their wage expectations. Lower major satisfaction and higher perceived difficulty go with stronger attrition intentions; programming and security-tool coursework is associated with higher attrition and vulnerability-detection coursework with lower. Major satisfaction matters in both models, suggesting that the quality of major education matters more than simply expanding enrollment.

Analysis of the Temporal Effects of Start-up Support Policies: Focusing on the Time to BEP among New Firms

Youngju LeePaper
Journal of Technology Finance, 14(3), 41–62KCIin Korean

Does government start-up support help new firms stabilize faster? Using the nationally representative Survey on One-person Creative Enterprises and propensity score matching, this paper finds that supported firms reach their break-even point about three months earlier than comparable unsupported firms. The results point to the speed of early stabilization as a useful yardstick for start-up policy.

The Relationship Between Crime Perception and Online Shopping Behavior: A Study of Consumers in Seoul

Dooseok Jang and Youngju LeePaper
The Journal of Internet Electronic Commerce Research, 25(6), 125–141KCIin Korean

Using the Seoul Survey (2020–2022) and a two-way fixed effects model, we find that people who perceive a higher risk of violent crime are more likely to prefer online shopping. The effect is stronger among high-income groups, with implications for crime prevention and public safety policy.

Cost Structure and Its Influence on Consumer Decisions for Eco-Friendly Products

Youngju Lee and Dooseok JangPaper
Journal of Product Research, 43(5), 93–99KCIin Korean

We classify eco-friendly purchasing into cost-saving, utility-accompanied, and high-cost behaviors and study their determinants with the Korea Environment Institute’s National Environmental Awareness Survey panel (2018–2022). Women, older, and more educated respondents are more likely to buy green. Income matters differently by cost structure: reusable-bag use is unrelated to income, water saving peaks at a monthly household income of about 5.74 million won, and high-cost purchases rise steadily with income.

Impact Analysis of the Regularization Policy for Non-Regular Workers: A Dynamic Approach to Employee Welfare and Job Dissatisfaction

Dooseok Jang and Youngju LeePaper
The Journal of Social Convergence Studies, 9(3), 73–87KCIin Korean

We evaluate Korea’s 2017 policy converting non-regular workers to regular positions, comparing workers eligible for conversion with existing regular employees using the Callaway and Sant’Anna (2021) estimator. After the policy, workplace welfare rose by about 37.7% and job dissatisfaction fell by about 60%, with effects growing over time. The gains were concentrated in benefits used in daily life, such as paid leave and meal and vacation subsidies, with no significant effects on injury insurance or reward leave.

The Impact of Climate Crisis Perception on Transportation and Walkability Satisfaction in Seoul

Dooseok Jang and Youngju LeePaper
Journal of Climate Change Research, 16(3-1), 389–399KCIScopusin Korean

Using 65,052 responses from the 2022–2023 Seoul Survey, we build a climate crisis perception index by principal component analysis and relate it to satisfaction with public transportation and walkability in a two-way fixed effects model. People with heightened climate crisis perception are more satisfied with public transit and pedestrian environments, especially in the top income quartile, who are also less likely to use private vehicles; no such pattern appears in the bottom quartile. Perception does not affect satisfaction with nighttime walking safety.

A Study on the Impact of Perceptions of Social Inequality on Happiness, Group Participation, and Prosocial Behavior in South Korea

Youngju Lee and Dooseok JangPaper
GRI Review, 27(1), 271–299KCIin Korean

Using the National Assembly Futures Institute’s surveys (2020–2023) and two-way fixed effects models, we study how perceived income inequality relates to happiness, group participation, and prosocial behavior. Stronger perceptions of inequality are associated with lower subjective happiness, less group participation, and greater distrust of others and institutions. There is no significant overall relationship with prosocial behavior, but lower-income respondents are more sensitive: among higher-income respondents, prosocial behavior is unrelated to perceived inequality.
